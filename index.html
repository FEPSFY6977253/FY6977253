Abstract

This report examines the crash of Japan Airlines Flight 123 on 12th August 1985, which is the deadliest single-aircraft accident in aviation history. The investigation determined that the cause of the crash was a rupture of the aft (rear) pressure bulkhead, which had been improperly repaired following a tail strike that happened 7 years prior. This structural failure led to the loss of all hydraulic systems and therefore control of the aircraft. The report analyses the cause of the accident as well as evaluating the lessons that have been learnt since. This includes the safety regulations, maintenance procedures, inspection requirements and aircraft design practices. The findings demonstrate that the accident significantly influenced the modern aviation safety standards and contributed to reducing the likelihood of similar failures occurring in the future.

Analysis and Discussion

On the 12th August 1985 a Boeing 747SR-100 departed from Tokyo, travelling to Osaka with the flight code JAL123. This aircraft with the registration JA8119 has operated this is exact domestic flight hundreds of times before, but this flight was very different.

7 years prior to this flight the aircraft had experienced a tail strike when landing in Osaka (A tail strike occurs when the aft fuselage (tail) of an aeroplane comes in contact with the runway during either takeoff or landing (SKYbrary Aviation Safety)). The tail strike caused the aft pressure bulkhead, a structural component that seals the rear of the pressurised cabin, to be damaged and therefore required repair. The repair was completed incorrectly, with only one row of rivets, where the doubler plate requires two. As a result, the cyclic pressurisation loads were concentrated around the singular row and accelerated fatigue crack growth. This was also left unnoticed due to poor maintenance.

12 minutes into the flight the aft pressure bulkhead ruptured, which led to the failure of the Auxiliary Power Unit (APU) and the support structure for the vertical stabiliser. On top of this, all 4 hydraulic systems were severed, ultimately making the aircraft unstable and uncontrollable (since the hydraulic systems are responsible for manoeuvring the aircraft).

Despite the inability to control the aircraft, the pilots were able to keep the aircraft in the sky for 32 minutes until the plane crashed. 505 passengers and 15 crew died, leaving only 4 survivors. The medical staff found bodies with injuries that suggest they survived the crashed only to die from shock, exposure to low temperatures or injuries that, if tended to earlier, would not have been fatal. Yumi Ochiai recalled from her hospital bed that she saw bright lights and the sound of a helicopter, while hearing screaming and moaning that gradually died away during the night. Overall, it is believed that an extra 20-50 people could have survived had the search and rescue team been deployed sooner. (Job, Macarthur (1996). Air Disaster. Vol. 2. Aerospace Publications. pp. 136–153. ISBN 978-1-875671-19-9. & Magnuson, Ed (June 21, 2005). "Disasters: Last Minutes of JAL 123". Time. Retrieved 2024-01-05.)

Lessons Learnt

After the accident the data received from the CVR (Cockpit Voice Recorder) hinted at the effect of hypoxia on the pilots. The results were analysed to explore the collective speech metrics, characteristics of silent pauses, utterance and reported voice acoustics (Aviation, space and environmental medicine, 2009-03, Vol.80 (3), p.252-252. Aerospace Medical Association). The results from this test were deemed to be useful in understanding how hypoxia affects speech. The speech measures were made immediately after decompression, 15 minutes after and 25 minutes after. At 15 minutes the verbal output fell by 70%, and the silent pauses increased by 150% with pauses longer than 11seconds doubling. This strongly suggests that hypoxia was present since hypoxia is low levels of oxygen in body tissues and causes symptoms like confusion, restlessness, inattentiveness and poor judgment (Hypoxia: Causes, Symptoms, Tests, Diagnosis & Treatment & Cerebral Hypoxia - American Brain Foundation).

The cause for JA8119 to crash was the improper repair and insufficient maintenance on the aft pressure bulkhead. This is a cause that could be very easily prevented with better practice and that’s what the FAA planned to introduce. The FAA’s Airworthiness Assurance Working Group (AAWG) conducted two surveys that covered 1,051 repairs on 65 aircraft that had been retired. They found that about 40% of the repairs were adequate, with the remaining 60% requiring additional supplemental inspections (Air Safety Week, 2005-03, Vol.19 (9), p.1 – New York: Access Intelligence LLC). Following this survey the FAA introduced the Advisory Circular (AC) 120-73 “Damage and Tolerance Assessment of Repairs to Pressurised Fuselages”. The guidance of Ac 120-73 was that “no operator could operate a nominated aircraft” (including the Boeing 747-100) “beyond a certain number of flight cycles or 25th May 2001, whichever occurred later” (Air Safety Week, 2005-03, Vol.19 (9), p.1 – New York: Access Intelligence LLC). This significantly changed the way that engineers treat aircraft repairs, but it did not revolutionise aviation safety alone. Instead, there were many new regulations that came after, each of which enhanced the safety of aircraft and reduced the chance of a large accident occurring through poor maintenance.

The B747 empennage (tail) was designed so that a vent door would open if an event of excessive pressure differential were to happen the pressure would be relieved before structural damage is incurred. The rate of which the pressure was released from the bulkhead meant that the structures failed, the first one being the vertical stabiliser. All 4 hydraulic lines were lost when the torque box and rudders separated. The 4 systems are in place to provide redundancy to ensure safe flight, but the accident raised concerns since all four lines went to the torque box. The safety box suggested that the hydraulic system in the empennage should be modified to ensure redundancy and eliminate any vulnerability. (A85_133_137)
